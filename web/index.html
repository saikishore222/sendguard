SendGuard
Solana devnet program ↗

Hold a payment until the recipient proves it's them

Address poisoning is when a scammer sends you a tiny payment from an address that looks like one you've used, so it shows up in your wallet history. Copy it by mistake and the money is gone. One person lost nearly $50M this way in December 2025, and a Solana user lost $2.91M in November 2024.

SendGuard holds the first payment to an address on-chain. To claim it, the recipient needs the right wallet and a secret link from you. If nobody claims it in time, it goes back to you.

Is your wallet being targeted?

Paste any Solana address. This reads its recent public history and looks for addresses that imitate ones it has paid. It never asks you to connect or sign.

Known limits

  • If the scammer is the person you're talking to, you'd give them the secret.
  • Exchange deposit addresses can't claim, so they'd need a timed hold instead. That isn't built yet.
  • The lookalike warning is only a hint. The secret is what protects you.
  • This is an unaudited devnet prototype that handles SOL only. The demo wallets are public on purpose, and verified contacts are saved in this browser.
